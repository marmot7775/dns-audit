# Doc 105: the DKIM sweep is Sendermap's dictionary

Step 3 of folding Sendermap in. Steps 1 and 2 (PR #209) made the map
the source for the vendor panel, the DKIM card's provider names and
the PDF. The audit's own DKIM probe still runs from its own
dictionary, comprehensive_selectors.py: 1,130 vendor names, 46 ESP
names and 156 generic names, none with a source. The map probes
Sendermap's dictionary, 106 names, each carrying the vendor, how the
name is known and a live domain that publishes it. So every audit
probes DKIM from two dictionaries, prioritised by two vendor tables
(SPF_VENDOR_MAP and MX_VENDOR_PATTERNS in spf_intelligence.py
against the hints in Sendermap's vendors.json) that nobody keeps in
step. Of the audit's vendor names, 232 are not in Sendermap's sweep,
and most of them are guesses at what a vendor might call a selector
(activecampaign, mailchimp, zoom, yahoo).

This doc gives the audit's probe Sendermap's candidates and
Sendermap's order, and deletes the audit's vendor tables. The probe
mechanics do not change: waves in chunks of DKIM_WAVE_CHUNK on
_dkim_executor, the drop check, DKIM_MAX_FOUND, the key analysis,
the dangling CNAME classification, the canary, tested_count and
unanswered_count all stay as they are. The generic fallback sweep
stays as it is too. Running one probe instead of two (the map in the
DKIM slot of Phase 2, smart_dkim_check reduced to the fallback) is
Doc 106, once this has run in production for a while.

Line numbers are from main at 52dbbf5. Sendermap is pinned at 0.1.2
(requirements.txt 16); everything below exists in that release.

## Step 0, measure before deleting

dns_snapshots.py 123 to 129 stores every selector the probe has ever
found in production as record_type dkim:<selector>. Before touching
the dictionary, run on the droplet, against the live
dns_snapshots.db beside the code (dns_snapshots.py 20):

```
sqlite3 dns_snapshots.db "select substr(record_type, 6) as selector, count(distinct domain) as domains, min(timestamp), max(timestamp) from dns_snapshots where record_type like 'dkim:%' group by selector order by domains desc;"
```

Then, with sendermap.catalog.load().probe_selectors and the current
comprehensive_selectors lists in a scratch script, split the audit's
vendor names that are not in Sendermap's sweep (the 232) into two
sets: ever matched in production, and never matched. Put both lists
in the report, with the domain count per matched name. Do not list
the domains themselves in the report; they are visitor data. Neil
decides which matched names go to Sendermap's vendors.json, with
what proof.

The matched names stay in the audit's priority wave until they are
in Sendermap's catalog: step 2 puts them in LOCAL_SELECTORS. The
never matched names go.

## Changes, in this order

1. **The candidates and their order.** In smart_dkim_check
   (spf_intelligence.py 349), delete the import at 377 to 381, the
   vendor detection at 406 to 436, the cap at 438 to 439 and the
   ESP_SELECTORS union at 441 to 453. In their place:

   ```
   cat = sendermap_adapter.catalog()
   includes = parse_spf_record(spf_record) if spf_record else []
   ranked = rank_rules(cat.rules, mx_hosts or [], includes)
   priority_selectors = list(probe_order(ranked, (*cat.probe_selectors, *LOCAL_SELECTORS)))
   ```

   rank_rules and probe_order are in sendermap.selectors. rank_rules
   moves the rules whose hints match the domain's MX hosts or SPF
   includes to the front and drops nothing; probe_order lays the
   sweep out in that rule order, generic names last. The whole sweep
   is the priority wave. There is no cap: 106 names is close to what
   the 40 plus ESP_SELECTORS wave already sent (86), and the wave
   still goes out in chunks of DKIM_WAVE_CHUNK (649), so the thread
   arithmetic at audit_engine.py 4842 to 4850 and AUDIT_THREAD_CEILING
   (4861) do not change. Remove the max_selectors parameter and its
   docstring lines (365 to 366); grep for callers passing it first.

   The vendor tag on a found key (matched_vendor, 539 to 543, stored
   as vendor at 552) keeps its meaning, the domain's own MX or SPF
   points at this vendor, but comes from the matched rules:

   ```
   mx_set = frozenset(h.lower().rstrip(".") for h in mx_hosts or [])
   spf_set = frozenset(i.lower().rstrip(".") for i in includes)
   backed = {}
   for rule in ranked:
       if rule.hints.matches(mx_set, spf_set):
           for name in rule.shape.expand():
               backed.setdefault(name.lower(), rule.vendor)
   ```

   and matched_vendor = backed.get(selector). result_transformer.py
   7217 to 7228 already reads this as the third fallback behind the
   map's providers and the catalog's name table, so the card does not
   change. vendors_detected (385, 423) and discovery_method (388, 427,
   436) are read nowhere outside this function; delete both keys
   unless grep over the repo and tests finds a reader.

   Delete detect_vendors_from_spf (174), MX_VENDOR_PATTERNS (215),
   _VENDOR_NAME_TO_INFO (223), detect_vendors_from_mx (228) and
   _selectors_from_vendors (254). SPF_VENDOR_MAP (53) stays, because
   spf_execution_engine.py 12 and 29 read vendor and category from it
   for the SPF card; its dkim_selectors values are dead after this
   doc, so remove that field from every entry and nothing else.
   Moving the SPF card's vendor names onto the catalog is a later
   step, not this one.

   Update the imports in spf_intelligence.py and the tests in step 3,
   CLAUDE.md 16 and ARCHITECTURE.md 52.

## Verify

Full suite before and after, playwright included. Then on
production after deploy, for google.com, a Microsoft 365 domain and
a self hosted domain publishing default._domainkey (dig first),
compare the DKIM card before and after: same found selectors, same
key sizes, same providers, tested_count at or below 106 plus
LOCAL_SELECTORS for the first two, and the generic fallback still
reached for the third:

```
curl -s 'https://dns-audit.com/api/audit?domain=DOMAIN&scope=deliverability' | jq '(.checks[]|select(.name=="DKIM")) | .status, .tested_count, [.found_selectors[]? | .selector, .key_bits, .provider]'
```

Note on ranking: in 0.1.2 only Google Workspace (hints smtp.google.com
and _spf.google.com), Microsoft 365 and SendGrid carry hints, so a
legacy aspmx.l.google.com MX or a Mailchimp include ranks nothing
and the sweep goes out in catalog order. That is a Sendermap catalog
gap, not an audit bug, and it costs at most one extra chunk, because
the whole sweep is sent. Do not add hints on the audit side.
