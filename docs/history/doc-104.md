# Doc 104: grade p=none by the question the scope asks

Doc 103 grouped the scopes by the visitor's question. The grades still
answer one question, spoofing, under every scope. A p=none record is
amber everywhere, so a sender who picked Email Deliverability to ask
whether their mail will reach the inbox is told their DMARC needs
work, when p=none is what the mailbox providers ask of them. Google,
Yahoo, and Microsoft require bulk senders to publish DMARC with at
least p=none; none of them conditions placement on enforcement.
Enforcement is a spoofing question, and that is the Email Security
scope.

This doc regrades one fact, p=none, under one scope,
`deliverability`. Every other grade under every scope is unchanged,
and `complete`, `email_security`, and `dmarc` keep grading p=none
amber. The grade reads from the result's `scope`, which Doc 103 made
canonical, and the cache is keyed by scope, so the same domain can
hold a green card under one scope and an amber one under another
without either run seeing the other.

## The rule

Under `scope == "deliverability"`, a DMARC record whose effective
policy is `none`, published or inherited from the organizational
domain, grades `pass`. The reason the record is published, rua
reporting, subdomain policy, syntax, and alignment are graded as they
are today. A p=none record with no rua still grades `warn`, because
reports are how a sender finds legitimate mail that fails; the warn
comes from the rua issue, not the policy. Under every other scope
nothing changes.

## Changes, in this order

1. **The card.** `transform_dmarc` (`result_transformer.py` 2906)
   gains a keyword `scope=None`. Its three callers in
   `audit_engine.py` (5235 and 5752) and inside `result_transformer.py`
   (2931, 3035, 3046) pass it through. Where the status is set for a
   published p=none (3193 to 3199) and an inherited p=none (3100 to
   3101), a `deliverability` scope sets `pass` instead of `warn`. The
   verdict text stays as it is, and the card carries
   `graded_for_scope: "deliverability"` so the page, the PDF, and the
   tests can tell a regraded card from a pass earned under the other
   rules.

   The engine's policy issue at `audit_engine.py` 1590 to 1600 (DMARC
   policy is none, severity warning) is what the card's details list
   shows. Under `deliverability` `transform_dmarc` lowers that one
   issue's severity to `info` and leaves its text and fix alone, so
   the details still say what p=none means and how to move on, as a
   note rather than a warning. The rua issue keeps its severity.

   Add one detail line to the card under `deliverability`, in the
   same shape as the existing detail items, placed first:
   Mailbox providers require bulk senders to publish DMARC, and
   p=none meets that. Blocking forged mail is graded under Email
   Security.

2. **The summary.** `build_executive_summary` (488) and
   `build_security_roadmap` (1403) gain a keyword `scope=None`, passed
   from `audit_engine.py` 6221 and 6250. Under `deliverability`:
   - The headline branch at 797 to 799 returns
     Your domain publishes the DMARC record mailbox providers ask
     bulk senders for. It does not block forged mail yet.
     with state `pass` and icon `shield-check`. Every other headline
     branch is unchanged, so a missing or broken record still fails.
   - The spoofing protection tile (849 to 855) keeps its amber
     Monitoring only label. It answers the spoofing question, the
     headline's second sentence says so, and relabelling it would
     claim protection the record does not give.
   - `deliverability_issues` (1025) does not add the p=none line, so
     `deliverability_summary` (1070 to 1071) does not name p=none as
     the top issue. The branches for a missing record, more than one
     record, and no SPF are unchanged.
   - The plan row Progress from p=none to enforcement (1583 to 1589)
     is not emitted. The rua row, the sp and np rows, and every other
     row are unchanged.

   The readiness scale inside the DMARC card's Details (5143, RFC
   9989 Ready through Misconfigured) is unchanged under every scope.
   It describes the record against RFC 9989, not a grade.

3. **The PDF.** `pdf_report.py` reads card status, the executive
   summary, and the roadmap, so it follows. Check that the cover
   counts, section 1 Summary, and section 2 What to do all agree with
   the web page for a `deliverability` run of a p=none domain, and
   that nothing in the PDF names p=none as a risk on that run.

4. **Tests.** One fixture, a zone with `v=DMARC1; p=none;
   rua=mailto:...`, aligned SPF, and a live DKIM key, audited under
   each of the five scopes:
   - `deliverability`: DMARC card `pass`, `graded_for_scope` set, the
     policy issue severity `info`, the new detail line first,
     `headline_state` `pass` with the sentence above, no plan row
     Progress from p=none to enforcement, `deliverability_summary`
     not mentioning p=none, spoofing tile label Monitoring only and
     color amber.
   - `complete`, `email_security`, `dmarc`: DMARC card `warn`, no
     `graded_for_scope`, headline state `warn`, the enforcement row
     present. These assert the regrade leaks nowhere.
   - The same zone with rua removed under `deliverability`: card
     `warn`, and the warn comes from the rua issue.
   - A subdomain inheriting p=none from its parent under
     `deliverability`: card `pass`, pill Inherited.
   - Cache: `/api/audit?domain=x&scope=deliverability` then
     `scope=email_security` return a green and an amber DMARC card
     for the same domain in the same five minutes.
   - PDF: the `deliverability` PDF for the fixture contains no
     Progress from p=none and its cover status counts match the web
     page's.
   Rendered checks in playwright: the `deliverability` results page
   for the fixture shows a green DMARC card and the pass headline,
   no console errors, and the Email Security page for the same data
   shows amber.

5. **CHANGELOG.md** entry in the style of the entries there: under
   Email Deliverability a p=none record grades pass, because mailbox
   providers require DMARC of bulk senders and do not condition
   placement on enforcement; the other scopes still grade it amber.

## Verify

Full suite before and after, playwright included. On production
after deploy, pick a public domain whose `_dmarc` TXT is p=none with
a rua (confirm with dig first), then:

```
curl -s 'https://dns-audit.com/api/audit?domain=DOMAIN&scope=deliverability' | jq '.executive_summary.headline_state, (.checks[]|select(.name=="DMARC")|.status, .graded_for_scope)'
curl -s 'https://dns-audit.com/api/audit?domain=DOMAIN&scope=email_security' | jq '.executive_summary.headline_state, (.checks[]|select(.name=="DMARC")|.status, .graded_for_scope)'
curl -s 'https://dns-audit.com/api/audit?domain=DOMAIN&scope=deliverability' | jq '[.security_roadmap[][]?.action?] | map(select(. != null)) | map(select(test("p=none")))'
```

Expected: `pass`, `pass`, `deliverability`; `warn`, `warn`, `null`;
an empty list. Open the home page for that domain under Email
Deliverability and confirm the green headline and green DMARC card,
then under Email Security and confirm amber. Download the
`deliverability` PDF and confirm section 2 has no p=none row.
